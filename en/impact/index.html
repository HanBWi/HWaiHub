Impact & metrics

What counts in the end: time, cost, lead time, error rate.

Every project starts with a baseline and ends with a measurement. We only roll out what demonstrably works – and you see it in numbers, not in slides.

Check your AI potential Book an intro call →

Measurement cycle

How a reliable result is created

  1. Capture the baselineHow long, how often, what does a case cost today?
  2. Set the goalA few metrics, agreed before the start.
  3. Implement in a limited scopeOne workflow in the pilot, with human approval.
  4. Measure afterwards & decideRoll out, adjust or deliberately stop.

What we measure

Six metrics – few, but reliable.

We choose two to four of them per workflow. You can see which measures are typical in your area under your situation.

  1. 01

    Time per case

    How long does a quote, an invoice, an answer take?

    How it's measuredSample before and after, with your key users.

  2. 02

    Lead time

    How long does a case take from receipt to result?

    How it's measuredFrom timestamps in email, ticket or system.

  3. 03

    Errors & rework

    How often does something have to be corrected, queried or redone?

    How it's measuredCorrections and follow-up questions per case.

  4. 04

    Degree of automation

    Which sub-steps does AI prepare or complete?

    How it's measuredPer sub-step of the workflow – before and after.

  5. 05

    Usage & acceptance

    Is the solution really used every day?

    How it's measuredActive usage and feedback from key users.

  6. 06

    Impact in euros

    What is the time gained worth?

    How it's measuredTime saved × internal hourly rate – calculated conservatively.

The same logic for every area: core metric before and after, trend around the start, degree of automation per sub-step – and the result in hours and euros.

FAQ

Frequently asked questions

Are the figures in the cockpit and the detailed reports real customer data?

No. They are concept visuals with example values from typical workflows in mid-sized companies. They show how we present impact – not which result you can expect.

Which metrics do you measure?

A few meaningful ones per workflow: for example time per case, lead time, error and rework rate, degree of automation per sub-step and the impact in euros – calculated conservatively. We agree on which ones fit together before the start.

When can you see whether something has improved?

We measure the baseline before the start and again after the pilot. We define the period upfront; in the examples it is 90 days after the start.

What happens if a pilot does not show the hoped-for impact?

Then you decide based on the measurement: adjust or deliberately stop. That is exactly why we start small – before larger sums are invested.

Which number would you like to see change in 90 days?

Tell us the process – we tell you what can realistically be measured.
